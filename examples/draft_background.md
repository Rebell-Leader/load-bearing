# Draft: background section (from a research assistant)

Soluble amyloid-β (Aβ) oligomers, rather than plaques, are now widely considered the proximate cause
of synaptic failure in Alzheimer's disease [cline2018; selkoe2016]. Naturally secreted oligomers inhibit
hippocampal long-term potentiation in vivo [walsh2002], and Aβ dimers isolated directly from Alzheimer's
brains impair synaptic plasticity and memory [shankar2008]. A specific 56-kDa assembly, Aβ*56, was shown
to impair memory in middle-aged Tg2576 mice and to cause memory deficits when purified and injected into
young rats [lesne2006], and comparisons of cell-derived and synthetic oligomers revealed assembly-specific
cognitive effects [reed2011]. Together, these findings motivate therapies that target defined oligomeric
species [larson2012].
